## 1.1 Lever Mechanism

### What is a Lever?

A lever is one of the six simple machines. It is a rigid bar that rotates around a fixed point called the fulcrum. It helps lift or move loads using less effort. Common examples include scissors, pliers, seesaws, bottle openers, wheelbarrows, and robot arms.

### Parts of a Lever

- **Fulcrum**: Pivot point.
- **Effort**: Applied force.
- **Load**: Object being moved.

![The three parts of a lever: fulcrum, effort, and load.](/img/mechanical/phase1-lever-parts.png)

### How a Lever Works

Applying effort on one side of the fulcrum creates a turning effect that moves the load. A longer effort arm reduces the force required.

### Mechanical Advantage

**Formula**: MA = Load ÷ Effort. **Example**: Load = 60 N, Effort = 20 N, MA = 3.

### Principle of Moments

Effort × Effort Arm = Load × Load Arm. **Example**: 40 N load at 10 cm balanced by 20 N effort at 20 cm.

### Class 1 Lever

Fulcrum in the middle. Examples: Seesaw, Scissors, Crowbar, Pliers. Robot Example: Pivoted gripper.

### Class 2 Lever

Load in the middle. Examples: Wheelbarrow, Bottle opener, Nutcracker. Robot Example: Lifting platform.

### Class 3 Lever

Effort in the middle. Examples: Human arm, Tweezers, Fishing rod. Robot Example: High-speed robot arm.

![The three lever classes, distinguished by where the fulcrum, load, and effort sit.](/img/mechanical/phase1-lever-class-3.png)

### Advantages

Reduces effort, can increase force or speed, simple, reliable, widely used.

### Disadvantages

Requires space, incorrect fulcrum reduces efficiency, pivot friction, can bend under overload.

### Applications

Construction, robotics, automobiles, cranes, industrial machinery, hand tools.

## 1.2 Wheel and Axle

### What is a Wheel and Axle?

A wheel and axle is one of the six simple machines. It consists of a large wheel rigidly connected to a smaller axle so both rotate together. It helps reduce effort, increase torque, or increase speed depending on its design.

**Definition**: A wheel and axle is a simple machine made of a large wheel connected to a smaller axle that rotate together to transmit force, motion, and torque.

### Parts of a Wheel and Axle

**Wheel**: Larger circular part that provides leverage. Functions: Reduces effort, increases distance traveled, improves efficiency.

**Axle**: Smaller shaft connected to the wheel. Functions: Supports the wheel, transfers rotational motion, delivers torque.

![The wheel and axle, rigidly connected so both rotate together.](/img/mechanical/phase1-wheel-and-axle-parts.png)

### How It Works

When force is applied to the wheel, torque is created. Because the wheel and axle are fixed together, the axle rotates. A large wheel allows a small effort to create a larger turning force on the axle.

### Torque

**Formula**: Torque = Force × Radius **Example**: Force = 20 N, Radius = 0.25 m, Torque = 20 × 0.25 = 5 N·m

### Mechanical Advantage

**Formula**: MA = Wheel Radius ÷ Axle Radius (or Wheel Diameter ÷ Axle Diameter). **Example**: Wheel diameter = 40 cm, Axle diameter = 10 cm, MA = 4.

### Robotics Applications

Robot drivetrain, gears (wheel-and-axle with teeth), intake rollers, conveyor systems, winches, and drive shafts all use the wheel-and-axle principle.

### Advantages

- Reduces effort.
- Increases torque.
- Can increase speed.
- Smooth and efficient motion.
- Simple and reliable.

### Disadvantages

- Bearing friction.
- Needs lubrication.
- Larger wheels require more space.
- Components wear over time.
