Two chargers, one pocket-sized checker, and the habits that make packs last seasons.

## Two chargers — never swap them

Your kit contains **two different chargers**, and they are not interchangeable:

| Charger | Output | Charges | How to recognise it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Robot battery charger | **15 V 1 A** | The robot's 3S pack, via the pack's DC jack | Read the output printed on the charger body: "Output: 15V" |
| Controller charger | **12 V 1 A** | The Controller, via its DC jack | Output label reads "12V" |

:::tip[Label them on day one]
The two chargers look similar and their plugs may even fit each other's jacks — the output label is the only reliable difference. On the day you unbox the kit, wrap a piece of coloured tape around each charger and write **ROBOT** and **CONTROLLER** on them. Ten seconds of tape prevents a season of doubt.
:::

## Charging, step by step

1. Inspect the pack first: no swelling, no damage, connectors clean.
2. Pick the **correct charger** — check its output label, not its shape.
3. Plug the charger into the pack's DC jack (or the Controller's), then into the wall. Charge in the designated area, with someone present, on a non-flammable surface.
4. When the charger signals complete, disconnect. Do not leave packs sitting on chargers overnight.
5. Log it: date, pack number, and anything unusual. Your future self will thank you at the competition.

## The battery checker — your pocket instrument

Your kit includes a small **battery checker** with an XT30 connector. Plug the pack's XT30 into it and it instantly displays the pack's voltage — no robot needed. It is the fastest answer to the three questions you will ask most often:

- **"Is this pack charged?"** — check before strapping a pack onto the robot; ~12.4 V or higher means match-ready.
- **"Is this pack empty or is the robot broken?"** — when a robot won't power up, checking the pack takes five seconds and cuts the mystery in half ([Module 5.5](/phase-5-wiring-wireless-and-the-whole-robot/conclusion-and-next-steps)).
- **"Which of these packs should we use?"** — check all packs at the start of a match day and line them up from fullest to emptiest.

## Storage and health

- **Match days:** charge fully. **Long breaks:** store around half charge — Li-ion cells age fastest when parked at 100%.
- **Health check:** watch the robot's live battery-voltage telemetry while driving. A healthy pack dips a little under hard acceleration and recovers. A tired pack's voltage **sags deeply** under the same load.
- **Retirement signs:** noticeably shorter run time, deep sag, any swelling, or getting unusually warm while charging. When in doubt, show a mentor and take it out of rotation.

:::tip[Number your packs]
Give every pack a big written number and rotate them evenly — the battery checker makes the rotation honest.
:::
