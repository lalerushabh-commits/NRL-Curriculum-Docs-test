Nothing in this kit can hurt you if you follow a few rules. Here they are — learn them before Phase 3, because that is where the battery comes out.

## Li-ion battery rules

Li-ion cells store serious energy in a small package. Respected, they are safe and reliable. The rules:

1. Charge **only** with the matched charger — **15 V 1 A for the robot battery, 12 V 1 A for the Controller** — **only** in the designated charging area, and **never** unattended.
2. Never short the battery's connector — keep it away from loose tools, metal rulers, and paperclips.
3. Never puncture, crush, drop-test, or disassemble a pack.
4. A pack that is **swollen, hot, or damaged** goes straight to a mentor — it does not go back on the robot, and it does not go in a bag.
5. For breaks longer than a week, store packs around half charge, not full ([Module 3.2](/phase-3-power/charging-checking-and-battery-care) explains why).

## The power-on protocol

One habit prevents almost every burnt component in team robotics: **no robot is powered until a second person — a teammate or a mentor — has checked the wiring.** Before the switch flips, they walk the connections: battery seated, connectors clicked, nothing pinched, nothing loose on the bench. It takes thirty seconds. Make it a ritual.

## Everyday bench discipline

- Hold bare boards **by the edges**; boards travel in anti-static bags ([Module 2.3](/phase-2-your-hardware/using-and-caring-for-your-boards)).
- No food or drinks on the electronics bench. One spilled drink can end a robot.
- **Never plug or unplug the battery cable while the system is on — and never plug or unplug the motor driver's power while the system is on.** Switch off first, every time.
- Route cables before powering, not after.

:::warning[The one-second rule]
If you smell something odd, hear a new buzz, or see smoke or a flash: **switch off first, investigate second.** The main switch exists so that your first move is always the same, and always fast.
:::
