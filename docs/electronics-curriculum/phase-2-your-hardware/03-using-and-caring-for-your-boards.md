The Command Hub and Controller are the two most valuable items in your kit. Here is how to keep them alive for the whole season — and beyond.

Motors can be replaced in minutes and cables live in the spares box — but if the Command Hub or Controller dies, your robot is finished until a new board arrives. These two boards deserve their own set of habits. None of them is difficult; all of them matter.

## Why these boards deserve respect

Each board is a complete computer with dozens of delicate components — a radio, regulators, sensors, and hundreds of solder joints smaller than a grain of rice. They are built to survive robot life, but they are not indestructible: their enemies are **impact, liquids, static, heat, and wrong voltages**. Every rule below guards against one of those five.

## The Dos

- **Do mount the Hub properly** — bolted flat through its mounting holes onto the chassis, where it is protected from impacts and falling tools.
- **Do hold boards by the edges**, and carry them in their anti-static bags whenever they are off the robot.
- **Do keep the Controller's lanyard around your neck** while driving — nearly every broken Controller in team robotics is a dropped Controller.
- **Do power down before connecting or disconnecting anything** — ports, cables, sensors, everything.
- **Do keep the boards clean and dry** — a dry brush or dry cloth only; dust off gently before competitions.
- **Do use the silkscreen** — read the label before you plug. The right cable in the wrong port is how good boards die.

## The Don'ts

- **Don't let water — or any liquid — anywhere near the boards.** No drinks on the electronics bench, ever. A wet board must be powered off immediately and handed to a mentor; powering a wet board destroys it.
- **Don't place boards on metal surfaces**, and don't let loose screws, tools, or wire offcuts sit on or under a powered board — one stray screw can short a dozen connections at once.
- **Don't force any connector.** If it doesn't slide in with gentle pressure, it is the wrong connector, the wrong way, or the wrong port. Stop and look.
- **Don't touch component pins or press on parts** — the shiny metal legs and small chips are not handles.
- **Don't leave boards in a hot car or in direct sun** — heat ages electronics and warps plastic connectors.
- **Don't "test" anything by improvising wiring** that this book doesn't describe. If you're not sure, that is exactly what mentors are for.

:::keyidea[The board's best protection is a habit, not a case]
Notice that every rule above costs seconds and no money. Teams that finish the season with healthy boards aren't lucky — they simply do these small things every single time.
:::
